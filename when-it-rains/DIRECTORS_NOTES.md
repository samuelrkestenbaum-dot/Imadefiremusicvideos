## VERDICT
Yes — the story makes sense, and the core idea is genuinely strong: a man
chases almost-sightings of her through rain, and when he finally stops
chasing, the sightings stop with him. The ghost was the chase. That's a real
film. Right now it plays at DEMO level (section scores 5-7): the skeleton,
rhythm, and restraint are label-quality, but the engine — the chain of
almost-sightings — isn't legible on screen yet, and the back half was planned
against the wrong clock. Fix the must-fix list and this is a LABEL-PITCH cut;
the full list gets it to release.

## WHAT'S WORKING (all six reviewers converge)
- Musical editing instincts: accelerating intro holds, J-cut vocal entrances,
  cuts on downbeats, the water/water image rhyme in CH1. Keep this grammar.
- Grief through objects and behavior, not acting: the unslept half of the
  bed, the mug returned, the head-bow, never showing her face.
- CH1's money shots: the rain-soaked hero on the 89.8 downbeat, the puddle
  face rippled apart, and the pastry-case reflection (genuinely fresh).
- The thesis and the escalation ladder; the PRE2 wrong-stranger valve; the
  mug reversal ending.
- The color law (cool = his world, warm = her). Canonize it in writing.
